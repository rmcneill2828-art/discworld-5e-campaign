## Established Canon

Facts this table has decided are true that aren't nailed down in the published companion docs — a street name, a shopkeeper, which noble family runs which racket, how a spell mishap actually looked. The Charter's own closing note says the setting firms up under pressure, in play — this is where that firming-up gets written down so it stays firm.

- Bob has an unprompted, compulsive habit of over-explaining himself — even (especially) when nobody's asked. Vimes has already clocked it.
- Bob's salute is broken — every attempt so far has come out as something else (a karate chop, a wave). Running physical gag; hasn't landed a clean one yet.
- Sergeant Colon exists, handled Bob's induction paperwork, and is exactly as thorough as "four hours of forms, no actual orders" suggests. Referenced, not yet appeared on-screen.
- Trolls (Detritus's clan, at least) dug much of the old stonework under Sator Square generations back — he knows how that ground is "supposed to sound."
- Bob's salute landed clean exactly once, making a formal arrest — notable specifically because every other attempt (four and counting) has failed. Not established whether that's a pattern (works under real pressure) or a fluke.
- Small Gods temple caretakers on the Isle resent Guild-funded or well-financed operations undercutting their honest collection plates — a real, ongoing point of friction worth remembering.
- Bob's Ideal ("the easy way, not the stupid way") isn't just flavor text — it nearly cost him Vimes's trust in his first real interview. The line between "good detective instinct" and "personal angle" is genuinely blurry for this character, on purpose, and Vimes now knows it.
- Bob's salute failed a second running time under Vimes's direct questioning (froze mid-motion, arm "fighting itself") — now four-for-five failed, one clean. Established pattern: it breaks worst under scrutiny from Vimes specifically.
- Bob talked his way past a counting-house clerk with a transparently bad cover story (failed Deception), but is demonstrably strong at direct-pressure social plays — landed a natural 20 flattering Trimble into an unprompted confession, and another natural 20 explaining the courier release to Vimes. His comfort zone is bluntness and leverage, not lies.
- Grindle & Sons, Counting (Guild Quarter) is the counting-house from Sallow's lead: run a year without its proprietor present, its real transactions hidden in a private blue-black-ink ledger authorized only by wax-sealed, unsigned orders.
- The wax seal from the Session 2 shrine chit and the seal on Grindle & Sons' transfer-order letters are a confirmed match: a coiled shape wrapped around a coin. Same operation, two locations.
- The mystery courier (unnamed) delivers sealed orders to Grindle & Sons every Wednesday past the ninth bell, never speaks, and wears a gold signet ring — dark stone, a bird with folded wings — that doesn't match any device Trimble recognizes from his own guild-seal experience. Read by both Trimble and Vimes as gentry or Guild-adjacent, not a hired hand.
- The Sweet Tooth, a confectioner's shop on Cheesemonger's Yard (off the flour market), is a second front for the operation — a silent woman receives courier deliveries there and issues chits, no names exchanged.
- Bob let the caught courier go free, uncharged, in exchange for the Sweet Tooth's address and his silence — a deliberate informant play, not an oversight. Vimes reviewed the call and endorsed it explicitly as "policework."
- Bob now lodges at Mrs. Palliser's boarding house, Cable Street, on Vimes's personal recommendation.
- Bob does not know what the Seamstresses' Guild actually is, asked Mrs. Palliser to recommend one to mend his socks, and — despite being told plainly what the Guild trades in — wrote in his own notebook that he intends to find out "what these mysterious Seamstresses repair." Running gag; already spreading through the Watch House grapevine via Mrs. Palliser's breakfast table, Colon, and presumably beyond. Bob caught himself just in time before reading that exact line aloud to Vimes.
- The Sweet Tooth's side-door exchange point (via a gap between buildings, not the front) is how sealed deliveries actually move — confirmed by direct observation, not just Trimble's account.
- The Coyne townhouse doorstep ironwork carries the same folded-wing bird device as the operation's wax seal — a public, if easy-to-miss, confirmation tying the family directly to both Grindle & Sons and the shrine chit.
- Alderman Josiah Coyne: licensing-board money, old counting-house family, guild-adjacent but never a Guild member outright, sat on the board roughly fifteen years per Vimes. Unseen in public for about a year — the same span Grindle & Sons has run without its proprietor present. Vimes flagged the parallel as deliberate, not coincidence.
- Vimes is treating the Coyne family as a case requiring real caution: warrants and records checked properly before any confrontation, specifically because a family with that much civic reach could make the case disappear if the Watch moves carelessly.
- Vimes's own stated philosophy, relayed by Detritus: a copper is never fully off duty, "'cause the city don't stop being the city just 'cause your shift ended" — but that doesn't mean no personal time exists, just that timing and judgment matter.
- The Seamstresses' Guild running gag has a genuine ending, of sorts: off duty, Bob attempted an amateur stakeout outside the Guild house (failed Stealth check — spotted instantly by a Guild doorkeeper who clocked his Watch coat under his coat) and, rather than finally absorbing what the Guild actually does, walked away more convinced than ever that they're simply excellent at sock repair. The doorkeeper, out of sheer professional exhaustion, actually mended his socks for four pence to end the conversation — meaning that, from Bob's honest point of view, he was right all along and has receipts to prove it. He now considers this settled fact and will cheerfully recommend the Guild for sock repairs, by name, to anyone who'll listen, entirely unaware of what he's actually advertising. Mrs. Palliser has given up correcting him and finds it more funny than worth the fight at this point.
- The promissory note in the Sweet Tooth's drawer is real leverage, not fiction: a debt against Edith Harrow's shop, held (never called in) by the Coyne estate, used to compel her cooperation for roughly a year. The same shape of leverage extends to her son Tomas via his position in the Coyne household.
- Coyne family property records show holdings — including Grindle & Sons itself — quietly transferred into Nevil Coyne's sole name at almost exactly the time Alderman Josiah Coyne stopped appearing in public. Vimes reads this as deliberate, not administrative housekeeping, and it's sharpened his suspicion that Josiah may no longer be alive, not merely absent.
- Bob and Detritus staged a loud, public "found nothing here" performance with Edith on the way out of the Sweet Tooth, specifically to protect her cover from anyone watching the street on the Coynes' behalf. Detritus played along; it held.

### Session 0 — Session Zero
2026-09-09

Solo table, one player. Built Bob NoLastname: Human Fighter, City Watch Recruit, Duelist fighting style. Skull Dice rolled Why 12 / How 17 — "one dropped coin from a life of crime, this city has the best rates" crossed with "talked his way onto a diplomatic escort meant for someone far more important." Using The Long Climb. No lines/veils raised; standard Pratchett register. Set Bob's arrival on the 3rd of Ick — Founding Night — as a light, unforced coincidence, not a planned hook.

### Session 1 — Founding Night
2026-09-09

Bob's first shift, four hours into the job, walked straight into Vimes at Pseudopolis Yard's Founding Night gathering — didn't recognize him, asked him outright what he was meant to be doing. Vimes assigned him to walk a beat with Sergeant Detritus. En route, asked a rat-catcher (Ratty Grouse) for directions and got a rumor along with them: an unheard-of god supposedly paying finder's fees for followers. At Sator Square, Detritus flagged a slow drain as wrong for the weather; Bob actually looked instead of guessing (rolled well) and spotted something deliberately wedged in the grate. Detritus pulled out a small, freshly carved idol. Offered the choice to investigate the Isle of Gods immediately or report back by the book — Bob chose to report. Vimes took the idol into his own custody, called it likely Isle of Gods business, and approved of the restraint shown. Good first night on the record, twice over.

### Session 2 — The Shrine
2026-09-09

Two days later, a University expert confirmed the idol matched no god on record. Vimes assigned Bob and Detritus to the Isle of Gods properly to find the shrine. They split up; Bob (badge already drawn, subtlety not attempted) knocked on a temple door and got Sister Marl, a pragmatic caretaker, who — despite his badly garbled questions — gave him the shrine's real location and a warning that "wanting numbers fast" wasn't about devotion. At the shrine, Bob tried to arrest the operator on a bribery charge that didn't hold up (Detritus corrected him on the spot), then was sent to search the shrine itself while Detritus questioned the man properly. Bob found a tally-board with a written quota and a chit bearing an unidentified wax seal. Shouting "Evidence" spooked the operator into running; Detritus caught him instantly. Bob was given the arrest himself — first clean salute of his career, badge and manacles both used correctly. Operator now in custody at Pseudopolis Yard, unquestioned. Detritus told him plainly that rank won't stay a formality if this keeps up.

### Session 3 — The Interview
2026-09-09

Same night. Vimes let Bob take the lead questioning the prisoner, now named Nathaniel "Faithful" Sallow — hired help, not the mastermind, paid two dollars a head for roughly two hundred signed converts. Bob asked directly how one could profit from the scheme; Vimes cut the interview off immediately and confronted him about it in front of Sallow and Detritus, treating it as a real, serious lapse rather than a joke. Bob recovered by reframing it as "following the money to find the organ grinder, not the monkey" — Vimes accepted it as genuine, once, and said so plainly: trust spent, not to be spent twice. Redirected properly, Sallow gave a real lead: the money arrives as fresh uncirculated coin via rotating anonymous couriers, last traced to a counting-house near the Guild Quarter using blue-black ledger ink. Vimes narrowed that to Merchants' Guild territory or its edge, called it tomorrow's work, and closed the interview.

### Session 4 — The Counting-House
2026-09-09

Next morning. Vimes assigned Bob and Detritus to the Guild Quarter to find the counting-house from Sallow's lead, telling them plainly the badge alone would spook it shut. Bob reported to Detritus, agreed to work it "the slow way," and paid a licensed Beggars' Guild pitch-worker outside Grindle & Sons, Counting for a lead — a solid Persuasion check bought a name, a pattern of oddly-rotating couriers, and coin that never showed wear. At the door, Bob's cover story (a bogus "security check") failed outright, but it didn't matter: it spooked a courier into bolting out the back, straight into Detritus, who'd been posted there on Bob's own call. The recovered bag held fresh, uncirculated coin. Bob leaned on the caught courier — an Intimidation check landed clean — and traded his freedom and silence for the Sweet Tooth's address (Cheesemonger's Yard, off the flour market) rather than booking him, judging an informant more useful than one more forgettable name in a cell.

Inside, senior clerk Obadiah Trimble initially stonewalled, then cracked wide open once Bob (flattery, natural 20) played to eleven years of unacknowledged pride: Grindle & Sons has run a year without its proprietor present, its real business hidden in a private transfer ledger written in blue-black ink and authorized only by unsigned, wax-sealed orders left in the letter-slot every Wednesday night. The seal — a coiled shape round a coin — matched the Session 2 shrine chit exactly. Trimble came in voluntarily with the ledger; Bob and Detritus brought him, the bag, and the evidence back to Pseudopolis Yard.

Reporting to Vimes, Bob's salute failed again under direct scrutiny, but his explanation for releasing the courier as an informant landed as genuine tradecraft (natural 20) — first unqualified approval from Vimes on record, explicitly framed as spending Session 3's rebuilt trust well. Vimes then personally re-interviewed Trimble with Bob sitting in; under Bob's silent, looming presence, Trimble gave a fuller description of the mystery courier — soft hands, good shoes, never speaks, a gold signet ring with a dark stone and a device of a bird with folded wings — read by both men as gentry or Guild money, not a street hire. Vimes queued a stakeout of the Sweet Tooth for first light and sent Bob to find lodging. Vimes personally recommended Mrs. Palliser's boarding house on Cable Street, where Bob settled in for his first real night's sleep in the city.

### Session 5 — The Coyne Name
2026-09-09

Next morning, over breakfast, Bob asked Mrs. Palliser to recommend a Seamstress to mend his socks — an escalating, running misunderstanding that ended with her spelling out the Guild's actual trade in plain terms, Bob writing an earnest note to "find out what they repair," and the story already halfway round the Watch House grapevine by the time he reached the Yard.

Detritus met him for the Sweet Tooth stakeout (Cheesemonger's Yard, off the flour market), briefing him to watch only, no knocking, after the Grindle's-door lesson. A strong Perception check caught the proprietress checking the street compulsively, favoring a hidden drawer, and glancing repeatedly at a gap between two buildings — a tell that paid off when a hooded courier emerged from that exact gap and made a silent, practiced exchange at a hidden side door. The party split: Detritus stayed to question the woman directly, while Bob tailed the courier (clean Stealth check) through increasingly respectable streets to a townhouse whose doorstep ironwork carried the same folded-wing device as the operation's wax seal.

Questioning a delivery boy on the street, Simnel Rudge, Bob learned the house belonged to the Coyne family — Alderman Josiah Coyne, unseen in public for roughly a year, and his son Nevil, who now personally handles deliveries and fits the courier's description exactly. Simnel agreed to watch the house and report anything odd to the Yard. Meanwhile, Detritus got nothing verbal from the shopkeeper but read her fear as genuine and likely coerced, not loyalty.

Back at Pseudopolis Yard, Bob staged a full theatrical reveal for Vimes — nearly derailed by almost reading his Seamstress note aloud, caught with a last-second recovery — before landing the Coyne name. Vimes recognized it immediately: licensing-board money, guild-adjacent, missing from public life the same year Grindle & Sons lost its own proprietor. He called it "properly good" work, ordered a careful, warrant-and-records approach rather than a direct confrontation given the family's civic reach, and approved Bob's own request for a warrant to search the Sweet Tooth's counter drawer — with explicit instruction to handle a likely-frightened, coerced witness gently. Both threads — the Coyne records and the drawer — queued for the next day's work.

Off duty that evening, Bob attempted his own amateur stakeout outside the Seamstresses' Guild, got spotted instantly by a Guild doorkeeper, and — rather than finally grasping what the Guild actually does despite it being spelled out for him twice now — walked away triumphantly certain he'd been right about the sock repairs all along, having talked (or exhausted) the doorkeeper into genuinely mending his socks for four pence. Reported his "victory" to Mrs. Palliser, who has given up correcting him.

Next day, warrant in hand, Bob and Detritus searched the Sweet Tooth's counter drawer and found a promissory note: proprietress Edith Harrow has been coerced for roughly a year via a debt held against her shop by the Coyne estate, with her son Tomas — in service at the Coyne house — as further leverage. Edith opened up fully once Bob responded with real empathy, revealing Tomas has noticed the old Alderman's rooms locked and untouched for a year and told her once he isn't sure Josiah Coyne is even still in the house. She offered full King's Evidence on one condition: Tomas comes out safely first. Bob and Detritus staged a loud "found nothing" performance on the way out to protect her cover. Back at the Yard, Vimes's own records check turned up a property transfer moving Coyne family holdings — including Grindle & Sons — solely into Nevil Coyne's name around the time his father vanished from public life. Reading the two findings together, Vimes ordered Tomas extracted from the household immediately, before Nevil has any reason to suspect Watch interest — session paused there, extraction plan not yet decided.
