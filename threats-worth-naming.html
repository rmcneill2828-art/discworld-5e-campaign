The Charter's whole approach is reflavor, don't rebuild — nowhere does that matter more than combat. Every entry below names a stock 5e statblock (Monster Manual unless noted) that already does the mechanical job; what you're changing is the description you read aloud when it hits, misses, or dies. Don't build new stat blocks for a home game unless you're genuinely enjoying the design work — the existing math is already balanced, tested, and one lookup away.

Party of four, standard difficulty math from the Dungeon Master's Guide, applies exactly as written — nothing here changes CR budgeting. What this page changes is which statblock you reach for and what you say about it.

Pacing a threat to the story The Charter's house rule is to pace by book, not by dungeon — one contained crisis per arc, not a room-by-room grind. Read CR loosely as a register, not just a difficulty knob: CR 1/8–1 is alley trouble, resolved in a scene. CR 2–5 is a named problem a Guild or the Watch would actually assign someone to. CR 6–10 is a citywide incident — the kind that gets Vetinari's personal attention. CR 11+ is cosmological, and should feel rare enough that the table sits up when it shows up.
On violence Pratchett's books contain real violence and take it seriously when it lands — but they almost never linger on gore for its own sake. Narrate a hit by its consequence (a dropped weapon, a shattered stall, a Watchman's whistle finally getting blown) more often than by wound detail. If a fight can end in a stand-off, a bribe, or someone getting genuinely talked down instead, that's usually the funnier and more Disc-flavored outcome — see Playing It Straight for the technique.

Shades Tough

CR 1/8–1/2

Not a professional. Just someone who's decided tonight's your bad night.

Statblock
Bandit (unlicensed) or Thug (means it)
Where
The Shades, the docks after dark, anywhere the Watch patrols in pairs or not at all
GM TipHave them ask for a "toll" before drawing anything — most Shades trouble would rather talk its way to your coin purse than actually fight for it.

Hostile Watchman

CR 1/8–3

Not a villain. A man with a whistle, a quota, and orders he half agrees with.

Statblock
Guard (rank and file) or Veteran (a sergeant who's seen worse than you)
Where
Pseudopolis Yard, any street the party has just made a scene on
GM TipA Watch encounter that ends in swords is usually a failure state for both sides — let Insight, a badge, or a name-drop of a Watch NPC the party knows end it before initiative is needed.

Guild Enforcer

CR 1–3

Here about your quota. Or your dues. Or a debt with someone else's name on it.

Statblock
Spy (Thieves'/Beggars', persuasive first) or Bandit Captain (Merchants'-adjacent muscle)
Where
Wherever the relevant Guild has a stake — see the Gazetteer
GM TipThey're collecting, not killing — give them a stated, negotiable demand before the first attack roll.

Guild Assassin

CR 8

An inhumation, invoiced. This one barely needs reflavoring — it's the same job either way.

Statblock
Assassin, unmodified
Where
Filigree Street's Guild House, or wherever the contract sends them
GM TipPlay them as immaculately polite before the ambush and utterly professional during it — the Guild's whole brand is that this was never personal.

Alchemists' Mishap

CR 1/2–2

Whatever it was supposed to be, it isn't that anymore.

Statblock
Gas Spore (drifting, volatile) or Magma Mephit (actively unstable and annoyed about it)
Where
The Guild Quarter, or anywhere a party's carrying something they shouldn't have taken from there
GM TipGuild Insurance (see the Guild Registration Dossiers) covers the property damage — make sure an NPC mentions the paperwork mid-fight.

Troll Muscle

CR 2–7

Slow and affable in the heat. A different creature entirely on a cold night.

Statblock
Ogre (a bouncer, a debt collector) or Stone Giant (genuinely dangerous, cornered)
Where
Doorways, building sites, anywhere hired muscle is standard fixtures
GM TipPurely narrated, never mechanical: let this one visibly slow down and slur mid-fight on a hot day, and snap sharper the colder the room gets. Culture, belief, and the history with dwarfs in Clan, Cave, and City.

Rogue Golem

CR 1–10

Doing exactly what the words in its head told it to. That's the whole problem.

Statblock
Animated Armor (small, workshop-scale) or Stone Golem (industrial, terrifying)
Where
New Brickfields building sites, a factory floor, a locked University storeroom
GM TipThe fix is almost never combat — it's finding and rewriting the words in its head. A fight with one is usually the party running out of time to do that peacefully.

Off-the-Wagon Vampire

CR 5–13

A Black-Ribboner who's fallen off it. This is the tragedy, not the twist.

Statblock
Vampire Spawn (young, still fighting it) or Vampire (lost the fight entirely)
Where
Biers, or wherever they've been hiding the relapse from their own community
GM TipLet the party meet them sober first, in an earlier scene, so the eventual fight costs something.

Unreformed Werewolf

CR 3

Everything Angua chose not to be.

Statblock
Werewolf, unmodified
Where
Uberwald plots, or a case that makes the Watch's own werewolf officer visibly uncomfortable
GM TipIf Angua's in the story, this fight is about her at least as much as it's about the party — let her reaction carry weight.

Nac Mac Feegle Mob

CR 1 (as a group)

Six inches tall, blue, and already fighting before you've finished the sentence "wait, who are—"

Statblock
Six to ten Pixies, stripped of their charm spells, kept aggressive and reckless
Where
Anywhere the joke needs a swarm of tiny, furious, weirdly honorable brawlers
GM TipPlay this entirely for comedy — huge numbers, minimal individual threat, and a code of honor the party has to actually learn mid-fight to end it cleanly. Clan structure, belief, and why the fight probably has a Kelda's blessing behind it: Answerable to No One.

Feral Swamp Dragon

CR 1/4–3

Bred as a pet, almost permanently ill, and one bad meal from detonating. Working as intended.

Statblock
Pseudodragon (still small) or a young Kobold-tier dragon reflavored larger, with its breath weapon renamed an involuntary detonation from indigestion or excitement
Where
A noble household's back garden, the Shades — see the Charter's Adventure Hooks for the missing-pet version of this
GM TipThe explosion should always be more property damage than casualty — swamp dragons are a running civic hazard, not an existential one. Full lore (diet, breeding culture, the collective noun) in Powers Worth Knowing.

Noble Dragon

CR 17–24

Summoned, not born — and it's wearing whoever called it like a distorted reflection.

Statblock
Adult or Ancient Red Dragon, reflavored: its flight and fire are magical rather than physical, and it visibly shares traits with whoever summoned it
Where
Nowhere ordinary — requires serious ritual and a magic-dense location. A campaign-defining event, not a random encounter
GM TipIt weakens the further it strays from its power source — the win condition is often severing that link, not out-damaging a dragon several tiers above the party. Treat this the way the Charter treats a sourcerer: rare enough that its arrival is the story, not an encounter inside one. Full lore in Powers Worth Knowing.

History Monk

CR 3

Manages the passage of time. Would rather you didn't notice the management.

Statblock
Master (Monster Manual), unmodified
Where
Wherever a scene needs to have technically already happened before the party arrives
GM TipBest used as a warning, not a fight — they intervene just enough to make a point, then leave before initiative would ever be needed.

Auditor of Reality

CR 6

Grey, precise, and would very much prefer this entire encounter didn't have to involve a person.

Statblock
Specter or Invisible Stalker, reflavored bureaucratic rather than hostile
Where
Nowhere obvious — that's the point. A slow-burn campaign antagonist, not a street encounter
GM TipNever let one raise its voice or its weapon first. Auditors act through rules, paperwork, and inevitability — direct combat should feel like their last resort, not their opener. Full lore in Powers Worth Knowing.

A Thing From Outside

CR 2–10

In its prettiest form, something like a cross between an ugly octopus and an angry bicycle. It does not get prettier from there.

Statblock
Half-formed breach: Gibbering Mouther (CR 2). Coherent incursion: Chuul or similar aberration (CR 4–5). Bel-Shamharoth-tier: Aboleth (CR 10), for a climax only — this one isn't meant to be won by combat
Where
Wherever magic's been used heavily, carelessly, or without rest — an overloaded ritual, a University exam gone wrong, a summoning circle drawn by someone who didn't finish reading the instructions
GM TipNever a straightforward fight. The correct play is almost always "stop the magic that's feeding it" or "close the breach," with any combat as a stalling action while that happens — more magic only makes it stronger. Full lore, what draws them, and an escalation table for the buildup live in Powers Worth Knowing.

A Small God

CR 1/4

A lizard the size of your thumb, shouting at beetles, absolutely certain it's still relevant.

Statblock
Sprite or similar tiny fey, reflavored as a god with no congregation left
Where
The Isle of Gods, or the Gods Quarter overflow — see the Gazetteer
GM TipNot a threat in combat terms — the "danger" here is entirely social: it wants believers, and it's not above manipulating the party into supplying some. For a proper pantheon and a Small God generator table, see Powers Worth Knowing.
