Money

Coin of the Realm

The Ankh-Morpork dollar (AM$) buys what a gold piece buys. Nothing about 5e's economy changes — only the name on the coin.

Keep every RAW price exactly as printed and just rename the denomination: 1 AM$ = 1 gp, 1 penny = 1 cp (100 pennies to the dollar), and silver "shillings" slot in at 1 sp = 10 pennies if your table wants a middle denomination. That's the whole system — you're reskinning currency, not rebalancing an economy.

Playing rich and poor The gap between a penny and a dollar is the gap between the Shades and the Isle of Gods, and it's worth narrating, not just totaling. A civilian PC feeling ten AM$ as real money, while a Guild factor barely notices it, is character work — let prices carry that weight in the fiction even when nobody's tracking encumbrance.

The Calendar

The months and the week below are the Disc's own — documented setting fact, not book plot. The specific dates worth marking on them are this table's.

How the Year Works

The working Discworld year runs 400 days across thirteen months, opening with the short month of Ick and running through to December. (The full celestial year is longer still — 800 days, with two of everything, summers included — but no table needs to track that in play; the 400-day version below is what everyone actually uses.)

The week runs eight days: Sunday through Saturday, then an extra day, Octeday, before the cycle starts again.

A note on ordering Even the source material isn't fully settled here — the books never state the month order outright, and some editions of the Discworld Companion and the Discworld Roleplaying Game open the year on Offle and close it on Ick instead of the other way around. This page uses the Ick-first, Hogswatch-anchored convention below because it's the more common one and it's easy to reason about at a table. Pick whichever convention you like; just stay consistent once you have.

Fixed Points in the Year

A handful of dates are pinned down by the setting itself. Use them as-is — they're good scaffolding precisely because they're already established, not because anything specific has to happen on them.

  • 1st of IckDiscworld canon
    Hogswatch Day

    The turn of the year. The night before is Hogswatch Eve; the city's version of a fresh start, kept by everyone from the Palace to the Shades.

  • 28th of AprilDiscworld canon
    Creator's Birthday

    A quieter, more contemplative date than Hogswatch — the kind of day Unseen University marks properly and everyone else mostly ignores.

  • Midyear, around JuneDiscworld canon
    Crueltide

    The exact midpoint of the working year. Superstition holds that luck runs backward on Crueltide — useful color, harmless to ignore mechanically.

  • High summer, exact date unfixedDiscworld canon
    Eve of Small Gods

    The first day of midsummer, and the traditional night for a new shrine to appear on the Isle of Gods — no one's ever quite sure who builds them.

  • 5th of GruneDiscworld canon
    Koom Valley Day

    A solemn historical anniversary, observed carefully by both dwarfs and trolls, for reasons that run deep and don't fully agree with each other. Handle it as real history with real weight — not as an invitation to restage anything specific.

This Table's Civic Dates

Everything below is original to this campaign — invented pressure and color slotted into the gaps between the fixed points above, not drawn from any book.

  • 3rd of IckThis table
    Founding Night

    The City Watch's own anniversary — old officers tell old stories at the Yard, and Vimes pretends not to enjoy it. A good night for a party member to be formally sworn in.

  • First week of OffleThis table
    The Guilds' Reckoning

    Every Guild's annual audit is due to the Patrician's clerks in the same week. Quota shortfalls, missing paperwork, and Guild rivalries all come to a head at once.

  • Mid JuneThis table
    Sator Square Fair

    Market stalls, unlicensed performers hoping the Fools' Guild is looking elsewhere, and a scaffold that, for once, isn't being used for its usual purpose.

  • Late AugustThis table
    The Alchemists' Open Trial

    One day a year the Guild demonstrates its "safest" experiments publicly. It has never once gone entirely to plan, and everyone attends anyway, for exactly that reason.

  • Early SpuneThis table
    Barge Day

    The first big trade barge of the season comes up the Ankh from the coast. The Docks are chaos, the Merchants' Guild is smug, and customs paperwork gets "lost" at a higher rate than usual.

  • SektoberThis table
    The Beggars' Muster

    Patch disputes across the whole Guild get formally re-arbitrated in one long, contentious sitting. Territory changes hands more in this one week than the rest of the year combined.

  • DecemberThis table
    Founders' Fast

    A day of civic quiet the University observes more strictly than the city does — libraries close, faculty go still, and something about Unseen University always feels very slightly wrong until sundown.

Table notes You don't need every date pinned before play — the fixed points above are safe to use immediately, and you can slot in or drop the invented ones as the campaign needs pressure. Once your table starts tracking an actual date, lock it in your campaign log so later sessions stay consistent.
