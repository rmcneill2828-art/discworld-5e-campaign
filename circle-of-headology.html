Druid Subclass

Circle of Headology

For witches who solve problems by understanding people, not transforming them.

Companion to The Discworld Charter and the Guild Registration Dossiers

A headologist doesn't cast a spell so much as arrange for you to believe the thing that's actually true.

Witches of the Ramtops have no hall, no dues, and no formal hierarchy — just reputation, a fierce sense of territory, and a talent for making the practical look uncanny. Most of their real work is done with a raised eyebrow, a well-timed silence, and an unshakeable read on exactly what a person needs to hear. The showy stuff — turning princes into frogs, say — is mostly a young witch's habit, and one the good ones grow out of fast.

Mechanically, this Circle is built for the Druid chassis: full Wisdom casting, and a signature ability that trades Wild Shape's transformation for something closer to what witches actually do in the books — sending their mind out into an animal while their body sits, undefended, exactly where they left it. Everything below plugs into standard Druid progression; nothing about the base class changes.

2nd Level

Headology Spells

Your instinct for what's really going on in a room shows up as an uncanny knack for information. You always have the following spells prepared, and they don't count against the number of spells you can prepare each day. If you gain access to a spell that doesn't appear on the Druid spell list, it's nonetheless a Druid spell for you.

2nd Level

Headology

You've learned to read a person faster than most people can lie to you. You gain proficiency in the Insight skill if you don't already have it, and you have advantage on Wisdom (Insight) checks made to determine what a creature wants, needs, or is afraid of.

In addition, when you make a Charisma (Persuasion) or Charisma (Intimidation) check, you can use your Wisdom modifier in place of your Charisma modifier. Headology isn't charm — it's telling someone a true, uncomfortable thing at exactly the right moment.

2nd Level — Replaces Wild Shape

Borrowing

You don't transform. You leave. As an action, you can touch a willing Beast you can see, or attempt to touch an unwilling one — an unwilling target can make a Wisdom saving throw against your spell save DC to refuse you — and send your mind into it, riding along behind its own senses.

While Borrowing:

  • Your own body falls unconscious where you left it, and remains that way — undefended, prone, and vulnerable to the unconscious condition's usual dangers — for as long as the Borrowing lasts.
  • You perceive everything the beast perceives, as though you occupied its space, and can communicate telepathically with it up to a mile away.
  • Once on each of your turns, you can suggest one action to the beast (move, attack, flee, fetch, and so on). The beast is not dominated — it acts on the suggestion only if the action wouldn't be obviously suicidal or contrary to its nature. A cat will investigate. A rabbit will not charge a wolf because you asked.
  • You don't gain the beast's statistics, senses beyond its own, or any of its special abilities beyond what its senses show you. You are a passenger, not the driver.

The Borrowing lasts for a number of hours equal to half your Druid level (rounded down), or until you end it early (no action required), or until your body takes any damage — which ends the Borrowing immediately and leaves you incapacitated until the end of your next turn as your mind snaps back.

You can Borrow twice, regaining all expended uses when you finish a short or long rest — identical to the standard Wild Shape economy. The Beast's Challenge Rating can't exceed the limit on the table below, using the same gating the base class already uses for Wild Shape.

Why this replaces Wild Shape rather than reskinning it Wild Shape's real function is giving a Druid a second, tougher body to fight in. Borrowing does the opposite — it's reconnaissance and influence, purchased by leaving your actual body exposed. Keeping the CR gate and the two-uses-per-rest economy from Wild Shape keeps this balanced against every other Circle without new math; the vulnerable-body clause is the trade-off that keeps it from just being a strictly better scouting tool.
6th Level

Second Thoughts

You've learned to watch yourself think, and to notice when something's doing the thinking for you. You have advantage on saving throws against being charmed or frightened, and against any spell or effect that would read your thoughts or alter your emotions.

In addition, when a creature within 30 feet of you that you can see is targeted by an illusion spell, a charm effect, or a fear effect, you can use your reaction to grant it advantage on its saving throw against that effect. Once you use this reaction, you can't use it again until you finish a short or long rest.

10th Level

Deep Borrowing

Your reach while Borrowing extends well past arm's length. You no longer need to touch a Beast to begin Borrowing it — you can target any Beast you can see within 60 feet, or any Beast you have Borrowed at least once before, anywhere on the same plane of existence, whether you can see it or not.

In addition, while you are Borrowing, you can extend your senses to one willing creature within 30 feet of your vacant body, letting it see and hear what you perceive through the beast — useful for a coven working a problem together, one mind out and one mind standing watch.

14th Level

The Look

You've spent a lifetime being underestimated and using it. As an action, choose one creature within 30 feet that can see you. That creature must succeed on a Wisdom saving throw against your spell save DC or become frightened of you for 1 minute. While frightened this way, the creature also has disadvantage on attack rolls against any target other than you — it can't quite bring itself to look away.

A creature that succeeds on the save is immune to this feature for 24 hours. You can use this feature once, and regain the ability to do so when you finish a long rest.

Table Notes

Prefer a Cleric? The Charter suggests either chassis works. To port this to a Cleric, drop Borrowing and its levels, fold Headology and Second Thoughts in as-is, and let The Look replace one Channel Divinity option at 14th level or later — a Domain built this way plays closer to a village priest than a hedge witch, which may suit a more god-adjacent table.

Balance note. Nothing above changes a number the core class already uses — spell slots, save DCs, and the Wild Shape economy are untouched, only retargeted. The only genuinely new risk is Borrowing's exposed-body clause, which is a cost, not a bonus, so it shouldn't need further tuning at most tables.
