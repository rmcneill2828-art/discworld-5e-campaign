Peoples of the Disc

Answerable to No One

The Nac Mac Feegle — six inches tall, already dead by their own reckoning, and not taking orders from anybody.

Companion to The Discworld Charter and Powers Worth Knowing

The Charter's species table lists "Gnome / Feegle" as one option for one row. This is what that row leaves out.

A plain 5e Gnome covers a Disc gnome fine if that's all a player wants — small, clever, done. The Nac Mac Feegle (sometimes called pictsies, and occasionally, respectfully, the Wee Free Men) are a specific people with their own cosmology, clan law, and grudge against authority, and they're worth the extra page if a player wants to actually play into it.

Where They Come From

Feegles didn't start on the Disc. By their own account, they once served the Fairy Queen — see Powers Worth Knowing's Elves & Fairyland section for her court — stealing and raiding across worlds same as any other of her subjects. What sets them apart is that they got uncomfortable with how she treated anyone weak or poor, rebelled, and broke away entirely, crossing over to the Disc as free agents who answer to no queen, no king, and no laird ever again.

That's the throughline worth keeping in mind: elves and Feegles share an origin and a rough size, and ended up as complete opposites. One embraced Fairyland's cruelty. The other couldn't stomach it and left.

What They Believe

Feegle cosmology runs backwards from everyone else's. They believe they're already dead, and that the Disc — this loud, brawling, sheep-stealing life — is the reward for it: a paradise they earned. What looks like dying, from the outside, is just a Feegle going back to "the Last World" — which, as far as they're concerned, is a duller, greyer place they're in no hurry to return to.

Why this matters at the table This is the actual source of Feegle fearlessness in a fight, not just a running joke — from their side, there's nothing to lose that they haven't already "lost" once. Play it with real conviction, not just as slapstick; the comedy lands harder when the belief underneath it is sincere.

Clan Structure

A clan is a family, a small nation, and a chain of command all at once, built around four roles:

The KeldaThe clan's mother and its actual authority — nearly every Feegle in a clan is her son, and her word is law by right of that. She rules; she doesn't fight.
The Big ManThe Kelda's husband and the clan's war-leader. He enforces her decisions and leads the fighting, but the decisions are hers — a Big Man who forgets that doesn't stay Big Man for long.
The GonnagleBard, historian, and diplomat in one small, loud package. He keeps the clan's whole memory in song, played on mousepipes, and is usually the one sent to actually negotiate with outsiders — a Feegle who can talk as well as fight is worth more than one who can only fight.
The ToadA once-human lawyer, transformed, now serving as the clan's dry, put-upon legal and practical advisor. Genuinely useful, endlessly unimpressed by everyone around him.

Succession, and Why New Clans Keep Appearing

Feegle daughters are rare — most of a Kelda's enormous brood are sons. When a daughter does come of age, she doesn't stay: she leaves to become Kelda of her own new clan somewhere else, taking a handful of brothers with her, usually including a gonnagle to keep the new clan's history from the first day.

GM Tip This is a built-in, no-explanation-needed reason for a brand new Feegle clan to simply exist somewhere the party didn't expect one — a young Kelda struck out on her own, and this hillside, cellar, or ruin was where she stopped. Use it any time you want Feegles in a scene without inventing new lore to justify it.

Everyday Feegle Life

Fighting, drinking, and stealing — specifically sheep — are the three pillars of an ordinary Feegle's week, roughly in that order of enthusiasm. Special Sheep Liquor gets a special mention: potent, homemade, and treated with the kind of reverence other cultures reserve for a fine vintage. Clans live underground, in warrens disguised as rabbit holes or worked into hillsides, and physically a Feegle is about six inches tall, red-haired, blue-tattooed, and often helmeted in a small animal skull — and, pound for pound, considerably stronger than that size has any right to suggest.

Table Notes

For a Feegle PC, a plain 5e Gnome or a homebrew Tiny race both work mechanically (Charter §VI) — the material above is what you reflavor onto that chassis, exactly like everything else in this repo. A lone Feegle scout, separated from their clan, is a good hook for a Feegle PC's own backstory; the First Day in the City pregen "Six-Fingers" Pell reads just as well as this with a one-line swap.

For Feegles as an actual encounter — usually a mob, rarely a single one — see Threats Worth Naming's Nac Mac Feegle Mob entry. If a specific clan (and its Kelda) becomes a recurring presence in your campaign, note them in your campaign log's Established Canon so the details stay consistent.
