Improv Tables

Word on the Street

Two d20 tables for the ninety percent of NPCs who don't have a name yet, and the rumors they carry.

Companion to The Discworld Charter and The Skull Dice

Names Worth Knowing covers the fifteen faces who matter. This covers everyone else — the ferryman, the clerk, the woman who's run that chestnut stall for thirty years and misses nothing.

Roll on Who's Talking when a player asks a stranger's name and you don't have one ready. Roll on Word on the Street when the party's fishing for a rumor, killing time in a tavern, or you just need the city to feel like it's doing something in the background. Use them together, use either alone, or ignore the dice and pick — same rule as every other table in this repo.

How to use this Roll a d20 on Who's Talking for a name and a reason they'd know something, then a d20 on Word on the Street for what they've actually heard. Not every rumor needs to be true — treat about a third of them as color or an outright red herring, and let the party's own Insight checks (or just good judgement) sort out which is which.

Word on the Street

d20They've Heard
1Someone's been asking about the party by name, and not paying for the information kindly.
2A Guild's short a member and isn't saying which one, or why.
3The Watch quietly reassigned a beat overnight, and nobody's saying which one or why.
4A shipment's gone missing from the Docks that officially never existed in the first place.
5There's a new face at the Mended Drum buying rounds and asking careful questions.
6A shrine on the Isle of Gods got more crowded overnight, seemingly on its own.
7Somebody's paying well above market rate for information nobody's supposed to have.
8An Alchemists' Guild "routine test" went louder than usual last night, and the Guild's being cagey about the cause.
9A noble family's swamp dragon is missing again, and this time nobody's laughing about it.
10Two Guild enforcers were seen arguing somewhere neither of them should have been seen at all.
11Someone posted a reward notice and then quietly took it back down before anyone could read the fine print.
12A University faculty member hasn't been seen at meals in three days, and the porters are being vague about why.
13A patch of Beggars' Territory changed hands overnight, and not through the usual channels.
14Somebody's forging Guild licenses — badly enough to be spotted, well enough that it's still working.
15A body turned up that the Watch logged as "natural causes" faster than anyone expected.
16A god nobody's heard of is supposedly looking for followers, and paying finder's fees.
17Someone important is asking after the party through a third party who won't give a name.
18A merchant's ledgers don't balance, and they're offering good coin to whoever can make them balance quietly.
19The Assassins' Guild has a contract out, and for once, everyone's guessing wrong about the target.
20Nothing. Absolutely nothing's happening, which several people find more suspicious than usual.

How It Plays Out

Two examples of the tables run together — a name, a trade, and a line of dialogue built on the spot.

Who: 5 · Word: 9

"Ratty" Grouse, between cellars, mentions offhand that a noble family's swamp dragon is missing again — and this time nobody's laughing about it.

Who: 20 · Word: 12

Old Mossop mutters that a University faculty member hasn't been seen at meals in three days, and the porters are being vague about why — which, coming from him, means something.

Reusing a name Any of the twenty on Who's Talking can recur — if "Ratty" Grouse turns out useful, there's no rule against him becoming a standing contact. If he's used once and never again, that's fine too; that's what he's for. Once someone from this table earns a real place in the story, move them into your campaign log under Names Worth Knowing — Status so they don't get overwritten by the next roll.
