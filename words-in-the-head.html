Peoples of the Disc

Words in the Head

Golems — sixty thousand years old, animated by scripture, and slowly buying themselves out of ownership one wage at a time.

Companion to The Discworld Charter and Powers Worth Knowing

A golem isn't a construct that happens to be alive. It's an argument about what "alive" even means, wearing a clay body and doing the washing-up.

The Charter's species table (§VI) points a golem PC at Warforged or a homebrew Construct race and moves on. This is the rest of it.

Ancient Origins

Golems are old — the first were shaped roughly sixty thousand years ago, long before most of the Disc's current civilizations existed, by a people called the Umnians. Those original golems were larger than the ones seen today, some ten feet tall, and more finely worked. Eventually, the priesthoods of the time forbade making any more, on the grounds that creating life is a god's business, not a craftsman's — which is exactly why every golem now walking the streets of Ankh-Morpork is, at minimum, thousands of years old.

Chem

A golem's animating force is chem — holy words, fired directly into the clay of its head, that don't just power a golem but define it. A golem with the word Freedom baked into its head will genuinely yearn for liberty, whether or not that's remotely useful to whoever's holding its ownership papers. Remove the chem, and the golem simply stops — no death, exactly, just an ending.

The exception worth knowing Golem legend holds that at least one golem has gone on functioning with no chem in its head at all — having apparently concluded that a golem's real self was never really in the words at all. "Words in the heart cannot be taken" is the line associated with it. Treat this as philosophy and rumor a golem character might genuinely believe or aspire to, not a rules exception to hand out — it's the kind of thing golems tell each other, the way any people tell stories about the one who got free.

Property to Person

For most of their working history, golems have been property, full stop — obedient without question, without pay, and without anyone much asking whether that obedience meant anything. That's changing, slowly and unevenly: golems freed from ownership have shown real personality and initiative, and a growing number now work for wages instead of an owner's convenience.

The clearest institutional expression of that shift is the Golem Trust, which hires out freed golems for paid work and puts the proceeds toward buying freedom for golems still owned by someone else. A golem literally working and saving to purchase itself is the setting's own answer to what a slave-narrative arc looks like when the "slave" in question is, on paper, furniture.

GM Tip This is a genuinely good arc for a golem PC without ever needing combat: how far along their own purchase are they, who currently holds the paperwork, and what happens to that relationship once the last payment clears? It's also a clean, sympathetic parallel to Reg Shoe's undead-rights activism in Names Worth Knowing — a different fight, the same basic question of who counts as a person.
