Session Zero

First Day in the City

Character creation, start to finish — six steps, one worksheet, no wrong answers.

Draws on The Discworld Charter, the Guild Registration Dossiers, The Long Climb, and The Skull Dice

You don't need six tabs open to build a character. You need this page, a d20, and about fifteen minutes.

Everything below is a fast path through character creation for this campaign specifically — full detail lives in the companion documents linked as you go, but you shouldn't need them to finish a first character. Follow the steps in order, fill in the worksheet at the end, and you're done.

1

Pick a Class

Any 5e class, unmodified. Nothing about the setting restricts what you can play, and nothing you pick here locks you out of any Origin in Step 3 — that's deliberate. Witch players should look at Circle of Headology, a Druid subclass built for headology and Borrowing instead of Wild Shape.

2

Pick a Species

Human, Dwarf, Troll, Gnome, Golem, a Reformed zombie/vampire/werewolf, an Igor — the Charter's Playable Peoples section has the full list with notes on 5e stat-block starting points for each.

3

Pick an Origin

Who backs you up: a Guild, the City Watch, Unseen University, or nobody — a Civilian. Mechanically this is your 5e Background, so pick one from the Guild Registration Dossiers for the skills, tools, equipment, and feature that come with it.

No wrong pairing A Fighter at Unseen University keeps every Fighter feature. A Wizard on Watch patrol keeps every Wizard feature. The mismatch only ever changes how NPCs treat you — never your character sheet.
4

Set Your Starting Rank

If your table is using The Long Climb, find your Origin's ladder and start at its bottom rank — Recruit, Prospect, Student, Apprentice, Prentice, Factor, First-Year, or Nobody in Particular, whichever applies. This holds true no matter what level you start the campaign at: everyone in this city is new here (see Step 5), so nobody's had time to earn a promotion yet. A level 5 character can absolutely still be a First-Year Student — that gap is a feature, not an error, and the Long Climb explains what to do about it.

Not using the Long Climb at your table? Skip this step — your character's title is just their class and level, same as any other 5e game.

5

Roll Your Reason and Your Route

Every character starts in Ankh-Morpork. Roll a d20 twice on The Skull Dice — once for why you're here, once for how you got here — or pick two results freely if the dice don't fit the character taking shape. Read them together as one line; that's your opening backstory, done.

6

Read the Room

Before your first session, skim the quick-reference checklist at the end of Playing It Straight — it's six lines, and it's the fastest way to get everyone's sense of the setting's comedy pointed the same direction before play starts.

Set the table's lines Pratchett's satire aims at institutions, tropes, and power — never at a person in the room. Take five minutes in session zero for the boring, standard, worth-doing-anyway check: anything anyone wants left out entirely (a line), and anything that's fine in short doses but shouldn't be dwelt on (a veil). Agree on a simple way to flag it mid-session — a word, a raised hand, whatever your table will actually use — and revisit it if the campaign's tone shifts later.

Three to Borrow

Pre-built starting points, if you'd rather adapt than build from scratch. Each shows the system doing something slightly different.

Constable Björn Ironfoot

Dwarf Fighter · City Watch · Level 3 · Rank: Recruit

Why
It's the only place your particular skillset is, technically, legal.
How
Escorted in by City Watch officers, under circumstances best left undiscussed.

Level 3 is enough to make Constable on paper. Björn hasn't put in for it yet — he's still deciding whether he wants the Watch to know his real name.

Agatha Nettlebed

Human Wizard · Unseen University · Level 5 · Rank: First-Year Student

Why
A god you'd never heard of told you, quite clearly, to go.
How
Talked your way onto a diplomatic escort meant for someone far more important.

Forty-one years old, unmistakably more capable than her classmates, and still assigned a seat in remedial thaumaturgy between two seventeen-year-olds. The proctors have opinions. She has not asked to hear them.

"Six-Fingers" Pell

Gnome Rogue · Civilian · Level 2 · Rank: Nobody in Particular

Why
You're one dropped coin from a life of crime, and this city has the best rates.
How
Won passage in a card game you're fairly sure was rigged, in your favor for once.

No Guild card, no patch, no patron — which means no quota either. Pell intends to keep it that way for as long as possible.

Before You Play

  • Agree what level the campaign starts at — every ladder in the Long Climb still applies at level 1 as easily as level 10.
  • Decide as a table whether you're using the Long Climb at all — it's fully optional, and the campaign runs fine without it.
  • Confirm at least one shared reference point for tone — read the Playing It Straight checklist together if nobody in the group has read the books recently.
  • Agree your table's lines and veils, and the signal for flagging one mid-session — see the callout in Step 6.
  • Let Origins overlap. A party that's one Watch recruit, one Guild thief, one civilian, and one wildly overqualified wizard student is the default shape of this setting, not an edge case.
