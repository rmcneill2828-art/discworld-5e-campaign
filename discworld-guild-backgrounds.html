Each dossier below is a standard 5e Background — two skill proficiencies, tool or language proficiencies, starting equipment, and one feature — reflavored for a Discworld Origin. Nothing here interacts with class. Take any dossier with any class; see the Charter's Origins section for how mismatches should play at the table.

Personality traits, ideals, bonds, and flaws are offered as a short curated list rather than full randomizer tables — pick one from each column, reroll nothing, steal freely.

Watchman

City Watch

You wear the badge. The badge, increasingly, wears you.

Skills
Athletics, Insight
Tools
One gaming set, thieves' tools (you know the tricks even if you don't use them)
Equipment
A badge of office, a set of manacles, a lantern, plain city-watch dress, a belt pouch (10 gp)
Feature — Official Business You can requisition minor Watch resources — a holding cell for a night, access to a crime scene, a runner to carry a message across the city — by invoking your rank. Overreach and your superiors hear about it. Off duty, most citizens assume you're still on the clock.

Personality

  • I write everything down. You never know what'll matter later.
  • I've stopped being surprised by anything.
  • I talk to the job like it can hear me.

Ideal

  • Law. The law protects everyone, even the people it's inconvenient for.
  • Order. A city that follows rules is a city people can survive in.

Bond

  • My commanding officer took a chance on me once. I don't forget that.
  • Someone I swore to protect, I didn't. I'm still making up for it.

Flaw

  • I follow procedure past the point it's helping anyone.
  • I trust the badge more than I trust the person wearing it.

Thieves' Guild Journeyman

Guild

Theft is legal. Theft without a license is the crime.

Skills
Sleight of Hand, Stealth
Tools
Thieves' tools, one gaming set
Equipment
A crowbar, dark hooded clothes, a Guild registration chit, a belt pouch (15 gp)
Feature — Guild License Theft committed within your quota and the Guild's rules of engagement is, on paper, legal. Caught within license, the Guild will vouch for you to the Watch — once. Fall behind on quota and it's the Guild, not the law, you should worry about.

Personality

  • I case a room out of habit, even ones I'm invited into.
  • I'm generous with what I take — spread it around, spread the risk.

Ideal

  • Fair Trade. Everyone gets robbed the same amount. That's the whole point of the quota.
  • Freedom. Rules are for people who need them.

Bond

  • My old mentor is doing time for a job I should've been on. I owe them.
  • I'm saving toward buying out of the Guild entirely.

Flaw

  • I can't walk past an unlocked door without trying it.
  • I'm behind on quota and it's starting to show.

Assassins' Guild Alumnus

Guild

Not murder. An inhumation — invoiced, and paid in advance.

Skills
Deception, Stealth
Tools
Poisoner's kit, one musical instrument (finishing-school manners)
Equipment
A set of fine clothes, a concealed blade, a Guild membership chit, 25 gp
Feature — Inhumation Contract You can have the Guild's clerks draw up a legitimate, invoiced contract against a target for a fee — slow, expensive, and untouchable by law. You can also, through the same clerks, learn whether someone else already has a contract out on a given name.

Personality

  • I have exquisite manners and no small talk.
  • I notice exits before I notice people.

Ideal

  • Professionalism. Do the job cleanly, get paid, go home.
  • Tradition. The Guild's rules exist for a reason — mostly to stop this becoming murder.

Bond

  • My Guild-house tutor is the closest thing I have to family.
  • I've never taken a contract on someone who didn't deserve it. I intend to keep it that way.

Flaw

  • I look down on anyone who kills without paperwork.
  • Old Guild-house rivalries still get under my skin.

Alchemists' Guild Apprentice

Guild

The eyebrows grow back. Eventually.

Skills
Arcana, Investigation
Tools
Alchemist's supplies
Equipment
A portable alchemy kit, a singed lab coat, a logbook of "promising" experiments, 10 gp
Feature — Guild Insurance Guild-registered mishaps — explosions, chemical fires, the odd collapsed wall — are covered. The Guild pays out for property damage caused in the course of documented research, no questions asked, within reason. "Within reason" is decided after the fact.

Personality

  • I narrate my own experiments out loud, mostly for the record.
  • I've been banned from three taverns for "testing."

Ideal

  • Discovery. Somebody has to find out what happens. Might as well be me.
  • Caution — theoretically. I believe in safety procedures. I just haven't gotten to them yet.

Bond

  • My old lab partner lost a hand to a formula I mixed. I owe them, permanently.
  • I'm chasing a formula my mentor never finished.

Flaw

  • I underestimate how much of anything is "too much."
  • I keep unstable samples on my person "for convenience."

Merchants' Guild Factor

Guild

Everything's negotiable. Even the things that aren't.

Skills
Persuasion, Insight
Tools
One gaming set, and one language of your choice
Equipment
A ledger, fine clothes, a signet seal, 20 gp in trade goods
Feature — Guild Credit In most cities the Guild trades with, you can arrange lodging, cargo space, or a modest loan through fellow members. Reputation is currency here — a factor who doesn't repay a favor doesn't get a second one.

Personality

  • I price everything in my head, out of habit, including friendships.
  • I remember every debt owed to me and every one I owe.

Ideal

  • Prosperity. A city that trades well is a city that doesn't need to fight.
  • Reputation. My word is worth more than my gold, so I guard it accordingly.

Bond

  • My family's trading house is one bad season from ruin. Everything I do is for it.
  • A rival factor ruined someone I care about. I intend to out-trade them into the ground.

Flaw

  • I'll take a bad deal rather than walk away from the table.
  • I assume everyone's angle is money, because mine usually is.

Fools' Guild Graduate

Guild

The joke has been pre-approved. The laughter has not.

Skills
Performance, Acrobatics
Tools
Disguise kit, one musical instrument
Equipment
Motley (folds down small), a slapstick, a Guild-issued joke license, 5 gp
Feature — Licensed Fool Your material is, technically, Guild-approved — which means you can say things in front of powerful people that would get anyone else arrested, provided you can produce the license and insist, with a straight face, that it was a joke.

Personality

  • I have never once landed the joke on the first try.
  • I take my act more seriously than anything else in my life.

Ideal

  • Truth. A joke can say what a straight sentence can't. That's the point of me.
  • Craft. Comedy is a discipline. I resent that nobody treats it as one.

Bond

  • My Guild examiner failed me twice. I'm still trying to prove them wrong.
  • The one time my material actually landed, it got someone in real trouble. I haven't been reckless since.

Flaw

  • I can't resist a bit, even when the room clearly needs me to stop.
  • I've forgotten how to say a serious thing seriously.

Beggars' Guild Licensee

Guild

Poverty, done properly, is a licensed trade.

Skills
Deception, Insight
Tools
Disguise kit, one gaming set
Equipment
A weatherproof coat with hidden pockets, a begging bowl, a folded Guild patch permit, a well-worn prop (a bandaged "wound," a sad puppet, a hand-lettered sign), 6 gp
Feature — Patch Rights You hold, or are working toward, recognized begging rights to a specific stretch of street. Other licensed beggars won't work your patch, the Watch is slower to move you along there, and you know that ground better than almost anyone — every doorway, every soft touch, every mark's schedule. Neglect it, fall behind on dues, or lose a Guild dispute, and you're back to unlicensed corners until you earn it again.

Personality

  • I know which doorway to sleep in before the frost does.
  • I never forget a face that's ignored me. Or one that hasn't.

Ideal

  • Solidarity. The patch system only works if everyone respects it. That's the whole Guild, really.
  • Survival. Nobody looks out for you at the bottom. I look out for me, and whoever's nearby.

Bond

  • An older beggar taught me the patter and left me their patch. I won't let it go to someone who doesn't deserve it.
  • Someone important walked past me once, before I had a name, and actually looked at me. I've never forgotten it.

Flaw

  • I still flinch when someone reaches into a pocket — good news or bad, I never know which.
  • I've got a sob story so well-rehearsed I sometimes believe it myself.

Unseen University Hanger-on

University

Technically enrolled. Technically.

Skills
Arcana, History
Tools
One language of your choice (fragments of Old High Nomic)
Equipment
A stack of overdue library books, ink and quill, a University scarf or pin, 10 gp
Feature — Library Privileges You can research in the University Library under supervision (the Library has its own opinions about who's welcome) and petition faculty for minor magical consultation. Replies are slow, often tangential, and occasionally useful.

Personality

  • I quote footnotes nobody asked for, mid-conversation.
  • I've been lost in the University stacks for entire afternoons and don't mention it.

Ideal

  • Knowledge. Somewhere in this library is the answer. I intend to outlast the search.
  • Belonging. I may not be faculty, but this place is mine as much as theirs.

Bond

  • A professor let me stay after I should've been expelled. I haven't figured out why.
  • I'm chasing a thesis nobody else at the University takes seriously.

Flaw

  • I namedrop faculty who wouldn't recognize me on the street.
  • I trust a book over a person's word, every time.

Civilian, Street-born

Unaffiliated

Nobody vouches for you. Nobody can hold anything over you, either.

Skills
Insight, Persuasion
Tools
One artisan's tools or vehicles (land), your choice
Equipment
Common clothes, a keepsake of no obvious value, a small debt to someone, 5 gp
Feature — Off the Books No Guild roster, Watch file, or University record has your name in it. You're invisible to quotas, licenses, and enrollment — which cuts both ways: no institution will back you up, but none can be turned against you either. People underestimate you right up until they don't.

Personality

  • I read a room before I read anything else in it.
  • I've learned to disappear in a crowd; I still do it without meaning to.

Ideal

  • Independence. Nobody's ever looked out for me but me. I intend to keep it that way.
  • Community. The street looks after its own, because nobody else will.

Bond

  • Someone fed me when I had nothing. I've never forgotten the address.
  • I'm still paying off the debt that's in my starting equipment. It's older than it looks.

Flaw

  • I don't trust an offer that doesn't have a catch, and I'm usually right.
  • I've stolen out of habit, even when I didn't need to.
